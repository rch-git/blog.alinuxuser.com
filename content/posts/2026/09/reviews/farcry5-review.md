_I finished this game on `Sunday, September 6, 2026, 7:02:00 PM`_

I started playing this shortly after I finished Far Cry 4. I think I enjoyed it more than Far Cry 4, but there are things it does that are far worse.

The game world is the best in the series. There are parts of the game that look photorealistic. There are vistas in Far Cry 5 which are truly beautiful, almost on par with Red Dead Redemption 2. There is a day and night cycle, but no weather changes, or different biomes. An argument could be made that this fits the lore because the entire story is set in one county in Montana. Why miss an opportunity to create a beautiful world because it does not conform to the most idiotic lore in the entire series? More on this later.

Weapon variety is quite good. Sniper rifles, shotguns, rocket launchers, grenades, rifles, and other very fun tools to cause chaos. They sound great too. I used the M1911 as my pistol of choice for the entire game because of how good it sounds, and how powerful it is. I also like that headshots are very powerful in this game. A well-timed headshot with the 1911 will down an enemy in most cases — except when the game decides to ignore its own rules. There is more customization than in previous games. The interface is very clunky for obvious reasons — it's made for consoles, and it doesn't translate well to PC.

The story has got to be the worst in the series. Joseph Seed is no Vaas. They want to pretend it is in the same league as Far Cry 3, but it isn't, and I am not obligated to find it interesting. They really tried to sell the idea that the main bad guys are this very charismatic and manipulative group of people that managed to brainwash people. We are supposed to believe that the cult took over an entire county in Montana, and the citizens are powerless because no one other than the bad guys owns guns. The cult is committing crimes like burning people alive and leaving bodies hanging on lamp posts Witcher 3 style but word never got around the country? No feds? No National Guard? Hope County is totally isolated from the rest of the world, but the cult has choppers, planes, a supply chain for drug trafficking, they are able to get gasoline for their transportation, all their trucks are branded, they are able to create uniforms with matching logos, and they can build massive monuments and an army, among many other things. It is so dumb.

Compare this to the simple story in Far Cry 3 - rich kid gets kidnapped by pirates/smugglers, he turns into a warrior and kills everyone. Is it perfect? No, but it is simple and convincing.

By far the worst thing about the game is the mission design. When you reach a particular level of resistance, or in other words, have annoyed the villain in the region to a certain point, you get ambushed and kidnapped, and the bad guy goes on some monologue. I am sure the game thinks it's communicating something deep with me, but all I could see was Ubisoft trying to recapture the magic of Vaas. Why they decided to interrupt the player in this manner, I will never know. It's extremely irritating.

The game is split into 3 regions, one for each of the siblings — the drug addict (Faith), the pastor (John), and the soldier (Jacob). I tried to pay attention to Faith, and see if I could understand what the heck she is trying to tell me, but none of it made any sense. The boss fights are extremely repetitive. You go down a bunker, fight a wave of enemies, and the game then makes you do something dumb to defeat the enemy, and it all ends with the bunker getting destroyed and the citizens taking back control of the region. There are also missions where you have to rescue 20 citizens who are being robbed or kidnapped by the cult, or destroy a certain number of trucks and boats carrying Bliss. These missions depend on the game spawning these events, and it's evident that they are done to pad gameplay time.

What Far Cry 5 does so well is creating a fantastic sandbox. The immersion comes from creating chaos. You have to make your own fun and create your own scenarios. It's a fun game when you play it like Just Cause 3. The shift in tone between the story and gameplay is jarring. Enemy AI is not very good; I feel like it shifts from good to comically bad.

I usually skip launch coverage for games I plan to play. If I want the experience, I want it unfiltered. So it was only years later that I learned some critics were furious that Ubisoft had not leaned harder into progressive politics. They wanted the game to lecture conservatives and spend its runtime explaining that the orange man was uniquely evil. The game nods in that direction in places. That was never going to be enough. Nothing short of being shouted at for the entire campaign would have satisfied them. I never understood the impulse. Why turn entertainment into a scolding? I have no interest in using a game to tell people they are wicked for voting the wrong way, and I have even less interest in being on the receiving end of such a lecture. These malcontents treat every story, every setting, every product as a vessel for their grievance politics. How dare you try to escape the real world and enjoy a video game after a long day at work? No fun allowed. Time spent trying to please them is time wasted. Their opinions should be discarded without apology. It is funny looking back from 2026. Ubisoft has no real principles. It will gesture just far enough to keep the sale, then move on. The last decade must have been a special kind of hell for Polygon and PC Gamer. In Far Cry 5, the flag-waving, gun-owning rural types are often the good guys. I can only imagine the disappointment among the holier-than-thou game journalists who wanted a sermon and got a video game instead.

Far Cry 5 is worth playing. It is a fun sandbox. If you can find it for less than $10, it is totally worth checking out.
