I beat this game 25 years ago. I still remember where I was when I played the last level. I had to refer to a walkthrough from Game Revolution to figure out some of the puzzles. I remember the game taking close to 24 hours to finish.

This was the second game I ever played. I owned a version of this game on a CD. Back then, it was perfectly normal for me to play a 3rd person game without the use of a mouse. Arrow keys, and Ctrl, Alt, End (mid air flip) were the only keys I needed. I cannot imagine playing this way anymore. I didn't know any better back then. The idea of aiming with a mouse was very alien to me. I had only played one other game at this point - Tomb Raider III.

I am going to talk about the game from the perspective of someone who played it back in 2001. When I first played the game young Lara missions, I thought it was the coolest thing ever. The levels in Cambodia (Angkor Wat) were amazing. I remember the level with the vibrant green pool, and it felt like video game graphics couldn't get any better. I played this game on a Intel Celron PC with 32MB RAM. There was no dedicated video card, 4GB hard drive on Windows 98. This was the peak of gaming for me.

Tomb Raider 3 was a tough game to start my Tomb Raider journey. I did not realize this at the time, but it is infamous for its difficulty. I managed to beat the game with the help of a walkthrough. I needed a walkthrough for the later levels of Tomb Raider 4. Most of the initial levels in TR4 were fine. They were a little difficult, but nothing crazy. Towards the end, especially levels with underwater puzzles, it was a total nightmare. Back then, without a video walkthrough, you had to read the walkthrough, memorize it to the best of your ability, and try to figure out solution to the puzzles. Sometimes, the walkthrough could describe exactly what is needed, and you could still get lost. The chess game, puzzles in the Giza levels were particularly difficult. Also Lost Library, and the Chamber of Tulun were particularly difficult. One of the things that made this game difficult is the amount of backtracking. Unlike the previous games, there is a significant amount of backtracking involved, and the game does not have a map as such, or any objectives. You load into a level, and you get to figure out what the game wants you to do. This was definitely very frustrating. Some of the puzzles are completely arbitrary. There is no way I could have figured out what the developers wanted to me to, there was nothing intuitive about the solution. You just had to know what you to do. Even if I tried guessing, I never would have guessed the solution. This is somewhat similar to the game design in Tomb Raider 3 - difficulty because of arbitrary puzzles.

Graphics for its time were excellent. It is noticeably better than Tomb Raider 3. This really _feels_ like a Tomb Raider game because of the setting. You are literally in tombs for the most part - a deprature from the previous two games, where the definition of a tomb was stretched very thin. Some of the levels are truly beautifully designed. Cleopatra's Palaces is one of the best. Burial Chambers is also a great level. That is a special level because it sets off the rest of the game.

Story begins with a young Lara Croft, sort of like an origin story of Tomb Raider. It then picks up in Egypt, and Lara Croft is tomb raiding, and she picks up the amulet of Horus, and this releases a curse. The rest of the game is spent trying to undo the damage. That's the extent of my understanding of the plot.

I thought they did an amazing job at the end. I think the game ends on the perfect note. I absolutely do not understand how they took this near perfect ending and came up with the story of Angel of Darkness, which is probably the worst mainline Tomb Raider game ever made.

Level design in this game ranges from perfection to awful. There is also the Times Exclusive level. This is a mini adventure that explores the Tutankhamun's tomb. The Tomb of Seth, Burial Chambers, Cleopatra's Palaces, and my least favorite are the Cairo levels. The rest of the levels fall somewhere between these two extremes.

I briefly played this game a couple of years ago, and it was rough. Its not unplayable, but I have moved onto playing modern games. Controls for games have been largely standardized now, and having to rewire my brain to play this was tough. The gameplay still holds up. I would recommend buying the remastered version of this game, if at all you are thinking about buying.

https://www.gog.com/en/game/tomb_raider_iv_vi_remastered

I would not pay the current asking price of $29.99. I would wait for a sale and pick it up for less.

I have such fond memories of this game, and for the most part they hold up. Control scheme is the hardest thing to get used to. I can probably breeze through the game now with video walkthroughs.

I would not say this is an all time classic, but its a very solid game. I do think this is better than Tomb Raider 3 though. The very first Tomb Raider is still the greatest game in the series, which is insane considering it was released back in 1996.

CGI cut scenes are well gone, but in game cutscenes did not age well. This is expecially evident in the Cairo levels. No Tomb Raider game is complete without mentioning the voice acting. Its very good. Jonell Elliott did a wonderful job. Hard for me to say which Tomb Raider had the best voice actor, but I think her voice suits Lara Croft better than the one from the reboot trilogy.
