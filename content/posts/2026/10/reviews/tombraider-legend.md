*Finished this game on Tuesday, April 30, 2019, 7:31:00 PM*

Tomb Raider: Legend is a special game for me. When the demo was released a little over 20 years ago, I remember waiting hours for it to download over my 48 Kbps internet connection. It was worth it. The demo was great and felt like proof that Tomb Raider had finally redeemed itself after the disappointments of Chronicles and Angel of Darkness.

I didn't buy the game at launch. Money was tight back then, and Tomb Raider gradually faded from my radar. My interest in the series wasn't reignited until 2019, when I picked up Tomb Raider (2013) during a sale. Before that, the last Tomb Raider game I had played was Unfinished Business in 2005. Returning to the series after a 14-year break felt good. Not long after finishing Tomb Raider (2013), I decided to finally play Legend, 13 years after its release. I was excited to experience the full game after all those years.

Tomb Raider: Legend is a soft reboot for the franchise, it wouldn't be the only time. The story revolves around King Arthur and ancient myths. I never fully understood all the details, but that didn't really matter to me. The gameplay and level design were strong enough to carry the experience. Even in 2019, the game had aged remarkably well. It ran without any technical issues, and most of my complaints were related to gameplay.

The biggest improvement over the classic Tomb Raider games is the control scheme. Legend feels much closer to a modern third-person action game, with a free-moving camera that gives the player far more control. The tank-like controls of the original games have been replaced with smooth, responsive movement.

The visuals and voice acting are also improved compared to the classic titles. The game takes place in a variety of locations - Bolivia, Peru, Japan, Ghana, Kazakhstan, England, and Nepal. That's an impressive amount of variety for a relatively short adventure. Nepal and Peru were the highlights for me. There's a good mix of exploration, puzzle-solving, platforming, and combat throughout the campaign.

They did a good job with the character model. It looks and plays well. Animations are very smooth, and they are not as slow as the classic games. This is one of the biggest complaints you hear about the classic games especially when someone plays them now, the controls feel very sluggish. I have to agree with this, but those games were a product for their time. Tomb Raider Legend introduced smooth animations, and platforming is fluid and fun. The levels and platforming are Legend's greatest strengths. Its a lot of fun. 

The part that I did not enjoy are the boss fights. They are all boring. The game does not let you be creative in anyway. There is only one way to defeat the boss, and often times it is arbitrary. This was my biggest problem with the game. You also have to rely on the dodge mechanic to complete to defeat the bosses - it was irritating in Legend, and it broke Anniversary for me. It was a dark time in video games where developers felt they needed to rely on what are essentially disguised quick time events. 

Its a fun game, and I would recommend buying this on GOG if you can find it for under $2. In 2026, I would not recommend paying more than that. There is not a lot of replay value - I know some folks would disagree because. I would not play this game for the story, but the gameplay does hold up, and it is a very solid Tomb Raider game.
