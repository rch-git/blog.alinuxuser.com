_I finished this game on `Saturday, August 3, 2024, 5:37 PM`._

This is the perfect 6/10 game. There are flashes of brilliance, but everything is just done by the numbers.

I played this game on PC (i7 4700k, GeForce 1070Ti, Windows 10). It worked fine on my 1920x1200 monitor. It is widescreen compatible from the get go. I did not have any technical issues or bugs, such as those related to framerate which are very common among console ports even to this day. This is one of those console ports that isn't a complete mess. The uncaged edition is the way to experience this game. In fact, I believe this is the only edition that is available on PC. I streamed this game on Twitch, and I think at one point in mine, I was the only in the world who was playing this game, which I thought was interesting. 

Console centric design is very obvious. The classics such as repetitive level design and enemies, quick time events and shallow RPG elements which exist just to pad the gameplay time. Its not terrible or anything. You get to decide what you want to upgrade, and unlock some moves and abilities. Its a very violent game, and once all the moves are unlocked, there is some fun to be had here. There are some combat sequence and finishing moves which are fantastic. If they embraced a more immersive design and did not resort by the numbers design choices, this could have been an amazing game.

Some boss fights such as the Sentinel fights are fun, but the Leviathan fights can go to hell. They weren't even fun the first time, let alone having to do them multiple times. It didnt take too long for me to finish the game. There are a couple of frustrating sections here and there, but overall, they managed to make this not irritating.

When the levels are not being repetitive, there are some quite interesting locations. The African levels can never be made today. Corporate will never approve this. Game journalists will feign outrage and everyone has to pretend it is a human rights violation. 

The game follows the movie plot. It tries to stay true to it, and they did a good job. There is some changes here and there, but they stuck to the events in the movie. You also get to fight the mute Deadpool as shown in the movie. I do like the developer's attention to detail and their commitment to the history of Wolverine. Loading screens have some very interesting trivia, and I enjoyed that. It was a nice touch. There is also a Portal reference. This was 2009. Orange Box was a cultural phenomenon. 

Voice acting is quite good. If I am not mistaken they had the cast of the movies do the voice work.

I played this at a time in my life where I was playing mediocre games. I was approaching my backlog all wrong. Life is too short, and I don't know how long PC gaming as a hobby is going to be around before everything becomes unaffordable, so I it is important to not waste time playing mediocre games. The best games in the backlog, regardless of how long it takes to play them should be tackled first. Greatness must be experienced fully. Only then, mediocre games should be played. 

Would I recommend someone to play this game in 2026? Well, assuming you can get your hands on this game, and you have no other games to play, sure. I think this is a better game than Ryse: Son of Rome. Would have been a great game if managed to free itself from the shackles of corporate imposed mediocrity.
