_I finished this game on `Saturday, August 3, 2024, 5:37 PM`._

This is the perfect 6/10 game. There are flashes of brilliance, but everything is just done by the numbers.

I played this game on PC. I put my retro machine, IvyBridge to good use. I streamed this game on Twitch, and I think at one point in mine, I was the only in the world who was playing this game.

This is a console game first. There are a lot of repetitive combat sections, and you tend to find the same enemies. The game follows the movie plot. It tries to stay true to it, and they did a good job. There is some changes here and there, but for the most part, they stuck to the events in the movie. The last first was pretty good. 

Some boss fights are fun. The boss fight with the Sentinel is fun as well. The uncaged edition is the way to experience this game. In fact, I believe this is the only edititon that is available on PC. 

Its a very violent game. It worked fine on my 1920x1200 monitor. Its widescreen compatible from the get go. I did not have any technical issues. I did play this on older hardware, but I was running this on Windows 10. 

Even though it is very console centric, it is not a terrible port. The part where the console slop shows up is the repetitive level design. The African levels can never be made today. Corporate will never approve this. Game journalists will feign outrage and everyone has to pretend it is a human rights violation. 

Voice acting is quite good. I did not encounter any bugs. There are some combat sequence and finishing moves which are fantastic. If they embraced a more immersive design and did not resort by the numbers design choices, this could have been an amazing game. 

There is an upgrade system because every game needs to have that. Its not terrible or anything. They unlock some moves and abilities, and you get to decide what you want to upgrade. This is a time when every game needed RPG elements. 

Level design is pretty good. There are quick time events. The fights against Leviathan are super boring. The first fight was fun-ish, but the rest can go to hell. 

It didnt take too long for me to finish the game. There are a couple of frustrating sections here and there, but overall, they managed to make this not a terribly frustrating game. 

I played this at a time in my life where I was playing mediocre games. I was approaching my backlog all wrong. Life is too short, and I don't know long PC gaming as a hobby is going to be around, so I it is important to not waste time playing mediocre games. The best games in the backlog, regardless of how long it takes to play them should be tackled first. Greatness must be experienced fully. Only then, mediocre games should be played. 

Some levels are better than others. I do like the developer's attention to detail and their commitment to the history of Wolverine. Loading screen has some very interesting trivia, and I enjoyed that. It was a nice touch. There is also a Portal reference. This was 2009. Orange Box was a cultural phenomenon. 

You also get to fight the mute Deadpool as shown in the movie. 

That's the issue with the game. Everything about it is good enough. Other than the Sentinel fight, which was memorable, its not a game that manages to free itself from the shackles of corporate imposed mediocrity. 

Would I recommend someone to play this game in 2026? Well, assuming you can get your hands on this game, and you have no other games to play, sure. I think this is a better game than Ryse: Son of Rome.
